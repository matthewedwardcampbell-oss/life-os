🏙️ City & Budget
Stay solvent while demand does the work

The RCI loop

  • Zone only what the bars ask for. Over-zoning gives you empty lots and zero tax — follow the RCI demand bars, not your dreams.
  • Demand has caps. Reward buildings raise them — chase the rewards that match what you're growing (parks and landmarks for wealthier residents, civic/commercial rewards for offices).
  • Desirability decides who moves in. Parks, trees, schools, health, clean air, short commutes pull in R$$$; pollution and long drives give you R$ tenements.

Money rules that keep you solvent

  • Fund the plant to the load, not the max. Query your power plant and set funding to ~20–30% above current usage. A new natural gas plant can run at ~20% funding for years.
  • Same trick everywhere: set school funding to actual enrollment, hospital funding to actual beds — never pay for empty capacity.
  • Services follow demand, not the other way round. School once you have families (~1,000+ sims), hospital later, police/fire placed by coverage radius.
  • Water can wait. Farms and low-density industry don't need pipes — delay the water grid until residential density rises and farms give way.

Taxes

  • 9% across the board is the safe default while a city is young.
  • Stimulate: drop one sector 1–2% to pull demand — dirty industry early, high-tech later once your schools produce educated workers.
  • Harvest: nudge R$$$ and CO$$$ up 0.5–1% once they're established. Watch the bars for a few months and back off if demand craters.
  • Move in half-steps. Tax changes take months to show up in demand — small moves, then patience.

Ordinances

  • Early game: skip anything with a per-capita cost — they eat a small city's whole margin.
  • Turn on the cheap demand-boosters and pollution reducers once you can carry them; they compound with desirability.
  • Late game, a rich city can afford the full slate — read each one's monthly cost before enabling.

🚇 Transit & Traffic
Commute time is the hidden tax

Commute is king

  • Sims tolerate up to ~150 minutes of commute — past that, buildings abandon. Watch the Commute Time graph like a hawk.
  • Route-query buildings to see where their workers actually drive; fix the path, not the whole city.
  • Short commutes raise desirability, which raises wealth, which raises taxes. Traffic is your budget.

Road hierarchy

  • Streets — cheap and slow. Use inside neighborhoods only.
  • Roads — your default grid.
  • Avenues — roughly double a road's capacity at higher speed. One avenue beats two parallel roads; use them as trunk routes and downtown spines.
  • Highways — regional connectors. Ramps only, nothing zones on them; don't thread one through downtown unless you mean it.

Layout that flows

  • Spine and ribs: an avenue spine, residential ribs facing the quiet side streets, commercial at the rib-ends so jobs sit on the spine.
  • Keep big intersections off the trunk route — offset T-junctions flow better than four-ways.
  • Red road → widen to avenue. Red avenue → add transit, not a second avenue.

The mass-transit ladder

  • Bus — cheap starter. Stations near homes and jobs, inside walking range of both.
  • Subway — the workhorse: invisible, huge capacity, no surface space. Expensive up front, wins at density.
  • Rail — best for freight (industry → edge connection or port) and intercity passengers.
  • Elevated / monorail — late-game luxury; the subway usually wins on cost per rider.
  • Ferries — slow. Fine for a scenic crossing, never your backbone.
  • Park-and-ride works: a lot or bus stop at the neighborhood edge, subway into downtown.

Read the data (Rush Hour)

  • Congestion view = how far over capacity a link is. Red means widen or give people another way.
  • Volume view = raw users per mode, morning vs evening — Sims don't always come home the way they left.
  • Cars pollute. Every rider you shift to transit is cleaner air and a shorter commute for everyone else.

🗺️ Custom Maps
Install new regions into the game

Where regions live

  • Windows: Documents\SimCity 4\Regions\<RegionName>\
  • Mac: ~/Documents/SimCity 4/Regions/
  • Each region folder holds config.bmp (the city-tile layout), region.ini, and the rendered city files once you start playing.
  • Download regions from Simtropolis (STEX) or SC4 Devotion — they arrive as a .zip with a config.bmp plus a grayscale terrain image, or as an .SC4M file.

config.bmp cheat sheet

Red · 1 px
Small city — 1×1 km tile
Green · 2×2 px
Medium city — 2×2 km tile
Blue · 4×4 px
Large city — 4×4 km tile
  • It's a plain 24-bit bitmap: one pixel = one kilometer of region. A 16×16 config = the classic 4-large-cities-across region.
  • Heightmap size rule: grayscale image = config size × 64, then + 1. A 16×16 config needs a 1025×1025 image.
  • In the image, black = sea level, white = peaks. Any pixel in config.bmp that isn't pure red/green/blue is left as open water/land with no city tile.

.SC4M files & making your own

  • Pre-rendered regions come as .SC4M — import them with the free SC4 Terraformer (SC4T), which saves the region into your Regions folder.
  • SC4T also converts an .SC4M into an editable grayscale image + config.bmp pair if you want to resize or tweak.
  • Your own terrain: any grayscale image works — hand-drawn, a desaturated photo, a real-world elevation export — as long as it obeys the ×64 + 1 rule.
  • After rendering, touch up for free in God mode before you found any city on the tile.

🌐 Regional Play
Optimum specialization for a regional city

Why specialize

  • Demand is regional, pollution is local. Industry anywhere feeds jobs region-wide — so put the filth where nobody rich lives.
  • Sims commute across borders through neighbor connections. One city's workers are the next city's workforce.
  • A region of specialists beats a region of all-rounders: every tile does one thing well instead of everything badly.
The archetype stack one role per tile
🏘️
Bedroom suburb
Mostly residential. Buys power, sends commuters out. Keep it clean, parked, and desirable — this is where your R$$/R$$$ live.
🏭
Industry hub
Dirty + manufacturing. The power-plant city, rail and highway to the edge, low taxes on industry. Nobody rich lives here — on purpose.
🌽
Farm belt
Small tiles of agriculture. Pretty and feeds demand, but barely pays for itself — don't expect farm taxes to fund anything.
💼
Downtown core
High-density commercial + offices. Only works once the region population is large enough to feed CO$$$ demand. Subway-fed, landmark-rich.
🔬
High-tech enclave
Clean industry. Needs educated workers (college-town neighbor) and low pollution. Park it next to the rich suburb, far from the industry hub.
⚡
Utility city (optional)
Power, water, waste for everyone. Overbuilt capacity funded by neighbor deals from the whole region.

Neighbor deals

  • You start them. In SC4 you control both cities, so you can open a power, water, or garbage deal any time.
  • Deals need the right connection to the border: power lines for power, pipes for water, a road link for garbage.
  • Canceling costs a penalty — and if the seller runs out of capacity, the deal auto-closes and you pay it anyway. Overbuild the selling city.

A region opening that works

1
Pick a city surrounded by neighbors. Pause the simulation to plan streets and leave open land for later.
2
In a corner: natural gas plant funded to ~20%, plus a small parcel of high-density industry for starter jobs.
3
Zone high-density residential, un-pause, and let it fill until demand tapers off.
4
Pause, replace the industry corner with residential — the region's industry hub takes over the jobs.
5
Save, exit to region, open the adjacent tile — that becomes your industry & power hub.
6
Connect the two: road + power line across the border, then start a power deal so the hub sells to the suburb.
7
Repeat outward: farms on small tiles, high-tech beside the rich suburb, downtown core last — once regional population can feed it.
