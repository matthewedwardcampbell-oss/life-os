Sel gris hard drops · electrolyte candy Denver · 290°F
French Celtic gray salt (your Celtic sea salt staple) as the mineral core — trace Mg/Ca/K/Fe/S, softer earthy bite. Eats like an isotonic sports drop.
⚗️ 3 quirks to work around
Moisture (5–8% in sel gris) → grind fine + dry at 170°F for 15 min, add off-heat, or it seeds grainy.
Clay haze → kaolinite/illite give an amber/gray opalescent cast (aesthetic — pairs with citrus/caramel).
Hygroscopic → Mg-chloride grabs humidity fast; airtight glass jar + an arrowroot/cornstarch dusting is non-negotiable.
🧂 Ingredients
Sugar matrix: 1 cup cane sugar · ⅓ cup light corn (or brown rice) syrup · ⅓ cup water
Electrolyte core: 1–1.5 tsp fine dry sel gris (~2,000–2,500 mg sodium/batch) · ¼ tsp KCl (NoSalt, optional — 4:1 Na:K) · 1 tsp citric acid (cuts salt, adds tart)
Flavor: ½ tsp–1 dram candy oil — lemon, grapefruit, blood orange, or peppermint
👩‍🍳 Method
1. Grind sel gris fine; if tacky, dry on parchment at 170°F for 15 min.
2. Dissolve sugar + syrup + water over medium heat, then stop stirring; boil undisturbed to exactly 290°F (hard-crack at Denver altitude).
3. Off heat, let bubbles settle ~30s, then rapidly whisk in the dry sel gris, KCl, citric acid, and flavor oil (stand back — hot steam).
4. Pour into silicone molds fast, before it drops below 260°F and seizes.
5. Cool 30–45 min to glass-hard, toss in ½ tsp arrowroot/cornstarch, seal in an airtight glass jar.
🎨 Pairings
Salted tart lemon / grapefruit + citric acid → authentic isotonic drop.
Smoky maple / butterscotch → omit citric acid, use maple or vanilla bean; the clay + minerals deepen the caramel.
